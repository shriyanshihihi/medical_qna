Migraine. Migraine is a recurrent headache disorder, typically one-sided and throbbing, lasting hours to days. It is often accompanied by nausea and sensitivity to light and sound, and some patients have an aura before the headache. Acute attacks are treated with simple analgesics or triptans. Preventive treatment may include propranolol or topiramate for frequent attacks.

Stroke. A stroke occurs when blood supply to part of the brain is cut off by a clot, called ischemic stroke, or by bleeding, called hemorrhagic stroke. Sudden facial droop, arm weakness and speech difficulty are warning signs, summarised by the FAST rule. An urgent brain scan separates the two types. Clot-dissolving therapy can be given to selected patients with ischemic stroke within a few hours of onset.
